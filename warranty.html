Evidence-backed risk transfer

A label with a consequence behind it.

For eligible transactions, the buyer can purchase a defined warranty: if the underlying label is revoked for a covered evidence failure within the warranty period, the warranty responds according to its terms.

Availability is evidence-dependent. This product requires a sufficient record of revocations, disputes and covered losses. It is quoted only where the data supports a price; it is not automatically attached to every label.

Structure

Evidence, decision and payment stay separate

The grade is never changed to improve warranty economics. The fee is agreed before issuance and never shares in a favourable conclusion.

01 · ELIGIBILITY

Only defined records qualify

Method version, evidence grade, shipment value, data completeness and exclusions are fixed before cover binds.

02 · COVER

A revocation trigger, not a satisfaction promise

The covered event and evidence standard are stated in the certificate. Commercial disappointment is not an evidence failure.

03 · CLAIM

Independent re-derivation first

The record, revocation reason and loss calculation are recomputed before any payment decision.

Pricing

The loss curve sets the price

Observed revocation frequency, dispute outcomes and severity by method, sector and evidence grade drive the rate. A counterparty cannot buy a better grade by buying more cover.

What this page does not do

This overview is not an insurance policy, binder or offer of cover. Eligibility, limits, exclusions, governing law, claims handling and any required underwriting or reinsurance partner are specified only in executed documents.

We do not pay claims merely because a buyer changed its mind or a regulator exercised discretion.

Potentially covered
  • Revocation caused by a defined evidence-chain failure
  • Undisclosed duplicate issuance attributable to the covered process
  • Material mismatch between the frozen record and issued label
Not covered by default
  • Market price, quality or demand changes
  • Regulatory discretion without a covered evidence failure
  • Supplier fraud outside the warranted evidence scope
